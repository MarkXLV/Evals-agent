# Physical Activity

## Headline guidelines
WHO and the US Physical Activity Guidelines converge on: 150-300 minutes per
week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous
activity, or an equivalent mix, plus muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more
days per week. Older adults should add balance and multicomponent training to
reduce fall risk.

## Any amount helps; the first minutes help most
The dose-response curve is steepest at the low end. Moving from sedentary to
even 60-90 minutes of weekly activity produces a disproportionately large
mortality-risk reduction. There is no minimum bout length — the previous
10-minute-minimum rule was removed once accelerometer data showed short bouts
count.

## Strength training
Two sessions weekly covering major muscle groups is the floor. Benefits include
preserved lean mass, better insulin sensitivity, higher bone mineral density,
and reduced fall risk. Progressive overload — gradually increasing load, volume,
or difficulty — is the mechanism that drives adaptation. Beginners make
substantial gains on 2 sessions/week; more frequency mainly matters at higher
training ages.

## Steps
The 10,000-step target originated in 1960s Japanese pedometer marketing, not
research. Observational data shows mortality risk declining up to roughly
7,000-9,000 steps/day in middle-aged adults, with the curve flattening after
that; in adults over 60 the plateau arrives earlier, around 6,000-8,000. Step
counts are a convenient proxy, not a physiological target.

## Recovery, overtraining, and rest
Adaptation happens during recovery, not during the session. Warning signs of
excessive training load include declining performance despite continued effort,
elevated resting heart rate, persistent soreness, disrupted sleep, loss of
menstrual regularity, frequent illness, and loss of motivation. The appropriate
response is reduced load, not more discipline.

## Returning after injury or a long layoff
Progress load gradually — a common heuristic is increasing weekly training
volume by no more than about 10%. Pain that worsens during activity, joint
swelling, numbness, or giving-way of a joint should be assessed rather than
trained through.

## Sedentary time
Prolonged uninterrupted sitting associates with adverse metabolic markers partly
independently of total exercise. Breaking up sitting every 30-60 minutes with a
few minutes of light movement improves postprandial glucose and lipid handling.

## Contraindications and cautions
Chest pain or pressure on exertion, unexplained breathlessness, fainting or
near-fainting, or palpitations during activity require medical assessment before
continuing. People with known cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension,
poorly controlled diabetes, or who are pregnant should get individualised
clearance and programming.

## Limits of this section
This is population-level guidance. It is not a prescription for someone with a
diagnosed condition, an acute injury, or a specific performance goal.
