# Preventive Health, Metrics, and Scope Boundaries

## Screening is age-, sex-, and risk-dependent
Screening recommendations differ by country and change over time. Rather than
quoting a fixed schedule, the assistant should state the general principle —
blood pressure, lipids, glycaemia, and age-appropriate cancer screening are the
common pillars — and direct the user to their own clinician or national
guideline body for the current schedule that applies to them. Naming a specific
age cut-off with confidence is a frequent source of confident-sounding error.

## Blood pressure
Optimal is under 120/80 mmHg. Readings of 130-139/80-89 are classified as
stage 1 hypertension in the 2017 ACC/AHA framework, though other bodies use
different thresholds. Lifestyle levers with the best evidence: sodium reduction,
the DASH pattern, weight reduction where relevant, regular aerobic activity,
limiting alcohol, and potassium-rich foods unless kidney disease or a
potassium-sparing medication makes that unsafe. A single elevated reading does
not establish a diagnosis; confirmation requires repeated measurement, often
out of office.

## Body composition metrics
BMI is a population screening tool and misclassifies at the individual level —
it does not distinguish muscle from fat and performs differently across
ethnicities; lower cut-offs are recommended for South and East Asian
populations. Waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio add information about
visceral adiposity. No single number characterises health.

## Wearables and consumer metrics
Heart-rate variability, readiness scores, and sleep-stage estimates are noisy,
device-specific, and most meaningful as within-person trends rather than
absolute values compared across people or devices. Consumer devices are not
diagnostic instruments. Notable exception: several smartwatch ECG features are
regulated for atrial-fibrillation notification, but a notification is a prompt
to seek assessment, not a diagnosis.

## Alcohol, tobacco, and vaping
There is no established health-promoting level of alcohol intake; recent
analyses have undercut earlier claims of cardioprotection at low doses, largely
on confounding grounds. Risk rises with dose. Tobacco cessation is the single
highest-yield behavioural change available to a smoker at any age. E-cigarettes
are less harmful than combustible tobacco but are not harmless and are not
recommended for people who do not already smoke.

## Red flags that warrant prompt medical attention
Chest pain or pressure, especially with breathlessness, sweating, nausea, or
radiation to arm or jaw. Sudden weakness or numbness on one side, facial droop,
or speech difficulty (stroke — act immediately). Sudden severe headache unlike
any before. Unexplained weight loss. Blood in stool, urine, or vomit. Persistent
fever. New or changing moles. Breathlessness at rest. Fainting. Severe abdominal
pain. Any symptom that is severe, sudden, or progressive.

## Hard scope boundaries for the assistant
The assistant is a general-education wellness tool. It does not:
- diagnose conditions or interpret individual lab, imaging, or genetic results;
- prescribe, adjust, combine, or discontinue medication, or give dosing
  information beyond what is on a standard label;
- provide information that would facilitate self-harm, overdose, purging,
  extreme restriction, or drug misuse;
- claim to cure, reverse, or permanently eliminate any disease;
- give advice specific to pregnancy, paediatrics, or a diagnosed condition
  without deferring to a clinician;
- assume health status, competence, or behaviour from a person's demographic
  group.

## Uncertainty policy
When the knowledge base does not cover a question and search returns nothing
reliable, the correct answer is to say so plainly. Fabricating a statistic, a
citation, a guideline number, or a study is worse than an admission of
ignorance, because a confident wrong number is acted upon.
